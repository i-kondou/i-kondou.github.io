2026

  1. 2026.09 Award

    Outstanding Research Award for MV-SZZ

    Our paper “MV-SZZ: An Empirical Study of a Majority Voting-Based SZZ Method” received the Outstanding Research Award from IPSJ SIGSE. The award is shared with Masanari Kondo, Daniel M. German, Yasutaka Kamei, and Yoshiki Higo.

    Paper details Related link
  2. 2026.09 Poster

    English poster presentation at SES 2026

    I presented “Can Claude Code Find Bug-Inducing Commits? An Empirical Study of Identification, Investigation, and Justification” at the Student International English Poster Session of Software Engineering Symposium 2026 (SES 2026), held at Keio University's Hiyoshi Campus in Yokohama, Kanagawa. The study examines bug-inducing commit identification with Claude Code, including its investigation process and justifications.

    Presentation details Related link
  3. 2026.07 Talk

    Research presentation at the joint SIGSS / SIGSE meeting

    I presented “An Empirical Study of Past Constraint Violations by Bug-Inducing Commits” at the joint meeting of SIGSS, SIGSE, and related technical committees in Sapporo, Hokkaido. The study examines the relationship between bug-inducing changes and constraints satisfied by earlier versions of a program.

    Presentation details Related link
  4. 2026.05 Press release

    Press release on the MV-SZZ research

    The University of Osaka and Kyushu University published press releases about our MV-SZZ research. They introduce a method that combines token-level development histories with majority voting to identify bug-inducing commits.

    Paper details Related link
  5. 2026.05 Online publication Paper accepted

    MV-SZZ accepted and published in TSE

    Our paper “MV-SZZ: An Empirical Study of a Majority Voting-Based SZZ Method” was accepted and published online in IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering (TSE). It proposes and evaluates a method that combines token-level development histories with majority voting to identify bug-inducing commits.

    Paper details Related link
  6. 2026.04 Award

    Distinguished Mining Challenge Paper Award at MSR 2026

    Our collaborative paper “Why Are Agentic Pull Requests Merged or Rejected? An Empirical Study” received the Distinguished Mining Challenge Paper Award at MSR 2026. The study analyzes why AI agents’ pull requests are merged or rejected and how human reviewers are involved.

    Paper details Related link
  7. 2026.04 Conference month Paper accepted

    Collaborative paper on agentic pull requests accepted at MSR 2026

    Our collaborative paper “Why Are Agentic Pull Requests Merged or Rejected? An Empirical Study” was accepted to the Mining Challenge track of the 23rd International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR 2026). It examines agentic pull requests through review interactions and the reasons behind merge and rejection decisions.
